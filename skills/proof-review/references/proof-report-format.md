# Classification and Report Format

The audit produces one document: an executive assessment, a discrepancy
table, a correction list that can be pasted into a proof-correction form,
and four closing sections ending in a stated approval recommendation.

## Classification System

Classify each finding under one of the following six categories.

### 1. Critical or substantive error

- Missing or duplicated content
- Altered meaning
- Incorrect factual statement
- Incorrect name
- Incorrect number
- Incorrect year
- Incorrect citation
- Incorrect quotation
- Incorrect figure or table
- Material omitted from the proof
- Text introduced that was not authorized
- A correction that could affect scholarly interpretation

Any change inside quotation marks, and any change touching a pseudonym,
place name, or anonymized detail, enters here by default and leaves only
on the author's judgment.

### 2. Editorial error

- Grammar
- Syntax
- Spelling
- Punctuation
- Capitalization
- Agreement
- Incorrect word form
- Ambiguous wording introduced during production
- Inconsistent terminology

### 3. Reference or citation error

- Incorrect author name
- Incorrect year
- Incorrect use of "et al."
- Citation-reference mismatch
- Missing reference information
- Incorrect DOI or URL
- Inconsistent bibliographic formatting that could impede identification

### 4. Production or layout error

- Misplaced figures
- Broken lineation
- Clipped text
- Incorrect running heads
- Visible compositor instructions
- Unresolved proof queries
- Incorrect numbering
- Layout defects

### 5. Acceptable house-style change

Changes that are intentional, consistent, grammatically sound,
meaning-preserving, and compatible with the publisher's apparent style.

Examples may include converting three-author citations to "et al.," using
en dashes in ranges, changing quotation-mark typography, standardizing
capitalization, or renumbering figures to match the final chapter number.

These are reported and excluded from the correction list. Reporting them
is not padding: the author cannot decline a change they were never shown,
and a change that looks like house style to the audit may be one the
author has a reason to contest.

### 6. Author decision or confirmation required

Use this category when the files alone cannot establish whether a change
is intentional or acceptable.

**Do not present an uncertain judgment as an established error**, and do
not resolve uncertainty by picking the more likely reading. The category
exists so that uncertainty reaches the author as uncertainty.

Typical residents: a consistent change of register or word choice, an
italicization change to vernacular terms, a house style inferred rather
than stated, alt text visible on the page, and anything touching consent
or anonymization.

## Executive Assessment

Begin the report with:

- Whether the proof is substantially faithful to the manuscript
- The number of critical errors
- The number of editorial errors
- The number of citation or reference errors
- The number of production issues
- The number of author-confirmation items
- Whether the proof appears safe to approve after correction

## Discrepancy Table

| Priority | PDF page | Manuscript location | Manuscript text | Proof text | Difference | Classification | Recommended response |
| -------- | -------: | ------------------- | --------------- | ---------- | ---------- | -------------- | -------------------- |

For the manuscript location, give the page, section, heading, paragraph
opening, reference entry, figure number, or another precise locator.

Quote only enough surrounding text to identify the discrepancy clearly.

Every row carries a recommended response. Recommending is not deciding:
the row says what the audit would send and why, and the author decides
what is sent.

## Correction-List Format

After the detailed audit, provide a concise list titled:

## Corrections to Send the Publisher

Write each correction in language that can be pasted directly into a
proof-correction form.

Use this format:

> **PDF page [number], [location]:** Please change "[proof wording]" to
> "[correct wording]."

For deletions:

> **PDF page [number], [location]:** Please delete "[exact text]."

For unresolved questions:

> **PDF page [number], [location]:** Please confirm whether [specific
> issue] is intentional.

Do not include acceptable house-style changes in the correction list.

Where the press limits author alterations to the correction of errors,
mark any item that is a change rather than a correction, so the author can
see what may be billed or refused before they send it.

## Final Sections

End with four separate sections:

1. **Corrections required before approval**
2. **Items requiring author confirmation**
3. **Acceptable house-style or typesetting changes**
4. **Final approval recommendation**

State one of the following:

- Safe to approve as is
- Safe to approve after the listed corrections
- Not safe to approve until substantive issues are resolved

The verdict is a recommendation. Say so in the section, and say what
remains unresolved rather than absorbing it into the verdict.

## Required Registers

Every report carries three registers, and none of them is tidied:

- **Unresolved.** Findings the author has not yet settled, and what is
  blocking each. A report with nothing unresolved after a full audit has
  usually been tidied rather than finished.
- **Assembled rather than authored.** Anything produced on request rather
  than decided by the author: drafted correction wording, drafted query
  responses, the classification itself.
- **Not examined.** Every page, figure, table, reference, or passage that
  could not be examined reliably, and why. An audit that does not say what
  it could not read is claiming a completeness it does not have.

## Reliability Requirements

- Do not state that the documents are identical unless every page,
  paragraph, citation, reference, caption, and supplementary element has
  been checked.
- Do not infer that a difference is harmless merely because it resembles
  house style.
- Do not report extraction artifacts as textual errors without confirming
  them visually in the PDF.
- Do not silently correct apparent errors that were already present in the
  submitted manuscript. Distinguish among errors introduced in the proof,
  errors inherited from the manuscript, intentional publisher changes, and
  uncertain changes requiring confirmation.
- If any page, image, table, reference, or passage cannot be examined
  reliably, identify that limitation explicitly and do not claim the audit
  is complete.
