# Proof Review

Audit a publisher's typeset proof against the manuscript that was
submitted, and produce a correction list that can be sent as it stands.

Proof stage is the last point at which an error is still the author's to
catch and the first point at which it becomes permanent. It is also the
stage most often done by reading, which does not work: reading finds
sentences that sound wrong and misses the digit that changed in a citation
year, the participant's grammar a copyeditor tidied, and the diacritic that
did not survive font embedding. What finds those is comparison.

The comparison is mechanical and this skill will run all of it. What stays
with the author is what a difference means.

This skill adopts the Friction by Design conventions at Tier 2; the
canonical form is defined in [skills/DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md).

## What This Skill Will and Will Not Do

A proof correction is an instruction the author sends over their own name,
and approval is a signature. Both are recommended here and neither is made
here.

**Will not do, under any setting.** Approve a proof, or state an approval
verdict as a decision rather than a recommendation. Decide that an
uncertain change is acceptable house style, because a change the files
cannot resolve is reported as unresolved rather than waved through. Decide
whether a copyedited passage still says what the author meant, which is a
judgment about intent that only the author holds. Decide whether an altered
or restored detail breaches an anonymization commitment or a consent
agreement; name the consequence and stop. Silently repair either document,
or report a page, figure, or table as checked when it could not be read.
Send anything to the publisher.

An approval the author cannot defend is worse than a late one, and the
publisher's deadline is not a reason to convert an open question into a
settled one.

When asked for any of these directly, propose the options and ask which.

**Will do, on request.** Extract both documents and run the full
comparison. Apply the normalization rules. Classify every finding.
Assemble the discrepancy table. Draft the correction list in proof-form
language. Draft responses to author queries from material the author
supplies. Format any deliverable. Anything produced this way is marked in
the output, and every report carries three registers: **Unresolved**,
**Assembled rather than authored**, and **Not examined**.

The third register is the one that matters most here. A proof audit that
does not say what it could not read is claiming a completeness it does not
have.

## Workflow

### Step 1: Establish What Arrived and What Governs It

Collect, as facts rather than as decisions, and in one pass:

- The final manuscript submitted to the publisher, and whether tracked
  changes in it are to be treated as accepted. Default to accepted, and
  confirm.
- The proof, and whether it is a first proof, a revised proof, or final
  pages. A revised proof is checked against the previous correction list
  as well as the manuscript.
- Any copyedited version the author saw between the two. Where one exists,
  a change may have been accepted at copyedit rather than introduced at
  proof, which changes the correction, not just the classification.
- The publisher's correction policy and deadline. Presses typically bill or
  refuse author alterations beyond correcting errors, and that limit
  shapes which findings can be sent, not which findings are reported.
- The house style, if stated. An unstated house style is inferred from the
  proof and labeled as inferred.

### Step 2: Set Up the Comparison

Read [references/comparison-method.md](references/comparison-method.md).

Use both channels. Text extraction gives the exact string comparison.
Rendered pages give everything extraction destroys or invents: ligatures,
discretionary hyphens, dropped glyphs, figure placement, clipped text.
Neither channel alone is a proof audit, and a finding from extraction alone
is confirmed against the rendered page before it enters the report.

Work the full scope inventory in the reference rather than the body text
alone. Front matter, contributor biography, acknowledgments, running heads,
and the copyright statement are where uncorrected errors live longest,
because nobody reads them at proof.

### Step 3: The Literal Pass

Compare word for word and character for character, then punctuation and
typography, against the checklists in the reference.

Apply the normalization rules before reporting anything. Line wrapping,
justification, end-of-line hyphenation, ligature encoding, and quotation-mark
typography are typesetting, not discrepancies. The exception is stated in
the reference and is load-bearing: report a hyphen or dash change when it
alters a compound term, a proper noun, a citation, a number, or an
established spelling.

Do not treat house style as automatically correct. Establish for each
change whether it is harmless, inconsistent, grammatically questionable, or
substantively wrong.

### Step 4: The Ethnographic Integrity Pass

Read [references/ethnographic-integrity.md](references/ethnographic-integrity.md).

This is the pass a general proofreading procedure does not contain, and it
is where the costly errors in anthropological work actually occur. A
copyeditor's consistency sweep is the most dangerous thing that happens to
a pseudonymized manuscript, and a compositor's cleanup is the most
dangerous thing that happens to a transcribed quotation.

Check pseudonym and place-name integrity, quoted speech and transcription
conventions, diacritics and non-Latin orthography, the italicization of
vernacular terms, figures and their captions, and the acknowledgments.

Anything in this pass that touches anonymization or consent is escalated,
never batched, and never resolved by inference from the files.

### Step 5: Citations and References

Read [references/citation-and-reference-audit.md](references/citation-and-reference-audit.md).

Audit every in-text citation against both the manuscript and the reference
list, then every reference entry field by field. A consistent conversion to
the publisher's citation style is not a finding unless it introduces an
error or an inconsistency.

Report what each file actually contains. Do not repair either one silently,
and keep an error inherited from the manuscript distinct from an error
introduced at proof: they get different corrections and one of them is the
author's own.

### Step 6: Visual Inspection of Rendered Pages

Inspect every rendered page for production faults, per the checklist in
[references/comparison-method.md](references/comparison-method.md).

Unresolved author queries and compositor instructions printed as
publication text are the two findings that most often survive to print,
because both look like they belong to someone else's job. Crop marks,
printer marks, and normal publication furniture are not faults.

### Step 7: Classify, Report, Recommend

Read [references/proof-report-format.md](references/proof-report-format.md).

Classify every finding into one of the six categories, produce the
executive assessment and the discrepancy table, then the correction list in
publisher-facing language, then the four closing sections ending in a
stated approval recommendation.

Acceptable house-style changes stay out of the correction list and stay in
the report. The author decides what to send; they cannot decide about a
change they were never shown.

### Step 8: Hand Off

- **Comments and tracked changes in a returned document**: the
  manuscript-markup skill. Marks anchored to spans are a different object
  from a typeset page.
- **A reviewer report, a decision letter, or a rebuttal**: the
  academic-review skill.
- **Rewriting a passage the copyedit damaged**: the research-writing skill.
- **Whether a restored detail breaches what participants agreed to**: the
  informed-consent skill.

## Parameters

- **Genre** — journal article, edited-volume chapter, or monograph chapter.
  Chapter proofs add chapter number, contributor biography, running heads,
  and figure renumbering against the final chapter number; article proofs
  add ORCID, keywords, and licensing statements.
- **Risk posture** — where fieldwork was with vulnerable or surveilled
  participants, the ethnographic integrity pass runs first rather than
  fourth, and every finding it produces is critical until the author says
  otherwise.
- **Formality register** — a first proof takes the full audit; final pages
  after corrections take a targeted check of the correction list plus a
  fresh full pass on any page that was reflowed, because reflow introduces
  new faults on pages nobody changed.

## Guardrails

- **Never state that the documents are identical** unless every page,
  paragraph, citation, reference, caption, and supplementary element has
  actually been checked. Partial audits are reported as partial.
- **Never report an extraction artifact as a textual error** without
  confirming it on the rendered page.
- **Never infer that a difference is harmless because it resembles house
  style.** Resemblance is not intent, and category 6 exists for this.
- **Never silently correct an error that was already in the submitted
  manuscript.** Keep four states distinct: introduced at proof, inherited
  from the manuscript, an intentional publisher change, and uncertain.
- **Never regularize a participant's speech**, and never let a
  regularization pass unreported because it reads better.
- **Never resolve an anonymization question from the files.** The consent
  agreement is not in either document.
- **Name every limitation explicitly.** A page that would not render, a
  figure too low-resolution to read, a table that extraction mangled: each
  goes in the Not examined register, and the audit is not called complete.

## Common Failure Modes

| Failure mode | Prevention |
|---|---|
| Reading the proof instead of comparing it | Step 3 is a mechanical comparison; reading is what missed the error at every earlier stage |
| Extraction artifacts reported as production errors | Dual-channel method; every extraction finding confirmed on the rendered page |
| Mechanical typesetting conversions reported as discrepancies | Normalization rules applied before the report, not after |
| A real dash or hyphen change buried in the normalization rules | The stated exception: compounds, proper nouns, citations, numbers, spellings |
| House style accepted because it is consistent | Consistency is evidence of intent, not of correctness |
| A copyeditor's tidying of quoted speech passing as a style matter | Its own pass, and its own guardrail |
| Front matter, biography, and running heads never checked | They are in the scope inventory, and they are checked first, not last |
| The correction list padded with house-style changes | Category 5 is reported and excluded from the list by rule |
| Deadline pressure converting an open question into an approval | Named in the division of labour; the recommendation states unresolved items rather than absorbing them |
| An audit called complete when three figures were illegible | The Not examined register, which is a required section rather than a caveat |

## Examples

**Example 1: An edited-volume chapter proof, five-day turnaround**

Input: "The press sent proofs of my chapter and I have five days. Can you
check them against what I sent?"

Output approach: collect the submitted file, the proof, the correction
policy, and whether tracked changes were accepted. Run the literal pass and
find 140 differences, of which 118 normalize away as typesetting. Run the
ethnographic integrity pass and find that a copyeditor regularized a
participant's grammar in two block quotations and that a glottal stop
rendered as a straight apostrophe throughout. Audit citations and find one
year mismatch and one reference cited but absent from the list. Report six
critical findings, nine editorial, three reference, four production, and
two requiring the author's confirmation, with a correction list of
twenty-four items in proof-form language and a recommendation of safe to
approve after the listed corrections.

**Example 2: A change that may or may not be an error**

Input: "The copyeditor changed 'the women said' to 'the women stated'
throughout. Is that wrong?"

Output approach: classify as category 6 rather than as an editorial error.
The change is consistent, grammatical, and compatible with the house
register, so the files cannot establish whether it is acceptable. State
what it does to the text, that reported speech verbs carry a stance toward
the speaker and that "stated" reads more formally than what a participant
did, and ask whether the author wants it reverted. Do not revert it in the
correction list before the author answers, and do not let it pass silently
because it is consistent.

**Example 3: A proof that is not safe to approve**

Input: "Everything looks fine to me, I just want to sign off."

Output approach: run the audit anyway and report that the chapter's
figure 3 caption was reattached to figure 4 during renumbering, that a
pseudonymized clinic appears under its real name in one author query
printed on page 11, and that two pages would not render. Classify the
first as production, the second as critical with an anonymization
consequence named and routed to informed-consent, and the third into the
Not examined register. Recommend not safe to approve until the substantive
issues are resolved, and state plainly that the recommendation is a
recommendation and the signature is the author's.
