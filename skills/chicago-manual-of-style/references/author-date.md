# Author-Date: Patterns for Anthropology

Chicago 18th-edition author-date, the default for anthropology venues. Check
`editions-and-venues.md` first if the venue has its own style sheet.

The examples are real anthropological works. The two journal articles were
rendered from their DOIs with the toolkit's formatter and corrected against
the artifacts described in `editions-and-venues.md`. The book details come
from the books. Do not reuse an example's page numbers for a different
citation.

## In the text

| Situation | Form |
|---|---|
| Basic, with page | (Geertz 1973, 12) |
| Author named in the sentence | Geertz (1973, 12) argues… |
| Page range | (Ortner 1984, 144–46) |
| Two authors | (Clifford and Marcus 1986) |
| Three or more authors (18th edition) | (First Author et al. 2019) |
| Several works, one parenthesis | (Marcus 1995; Ortner 1984), ordered chronologically or alphabetically, consistently |
| Same author, several years | (Ortner 1984, 2006) |
| Same author, same year | (Marcus 1995a, 1995b), with matching suffixes in the list |
| Reprinted or translated classic | (Mauss [1925] 1990, 33) |
| Organization as author | (American Anthropological Association 2012) |
| No date | (Author, n.d.) |

Put the parenthetical where the borrowed idea ends, before the period, or
directly after the author's name when the sentence goes on to other
material.

## The reference list

Alphabetical by first author's surname, hanging indent, headed "References
Cited" or "References" as the venue prefers. The year follows the author
names. An author's works run in date order; works with co-authors follow
that author's sole-authored works.

### Book

Geertz, Clifford. 1973. *The Interpretation of Cultures: Selected Essays*.
Basic Books.

No place of publication (18th edition). Give an edition after the title when
it is not the first: *Title*. 2nd ed. Publisher.

### Edited volume

Clifford, James, and George E. Marcus, eds. 1986. *Writing Culture: The
Poetics and Politics of Ethnography*. University of California Press.

### Chapter in an edited volume

Pratt, Mary Louise. 1986. "Fieldwork in Common Places." In *Writing Culture:
The Poetics and Politics of Ethnography*, edited by James Clifford and
George E. Marcus. University of California Press.

The 18th edition no longer requires the chapter's page range in the list;
cite specific pages in the text. If two or more chapters from one volume are
cited, a venue may let each chapter entry cross-reference the volume entry
(…In Clifford and Marcus 1986.); check its style sheet.

### Translated book, original date shown

Mauss, Marcel. (1925) 1990. *The Gift: The Form and Reason for Exchange in
Archaic Societies*. Translated by W. D. Halls. Routledge.

In the text: (Mauss [1925] 1990, 33). The original year tells the reader
when the argument was made; the second year identifies the edition whose
pages are cited. Some venues and publishers drop the original year; follow
the venue.

### Journal article

Marcus, George E. 1995. "Ethnography in/of the World System: The Emergence
of Multi-Sited Ethnography." *Annual Review of Anthropology* 24: 95–117.
https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.an.24.100195.000523.

Ortner, Sherry B. 1984. "Theory in Anthropology since the Sixties."
*Comparative Studies in Society and History* 26 (1): 126–66.
https://doi.org/10.1017/S0010417500010811.

Volume, then issue in parentheses where the journal uses issues, then the
page range after a colon. Annual Review volumes carry no issue number (see
the registrar artifacts in `editions-and-venues.md`).

### E-book or consulted online

Treat as the book, adding the format or the URL at the end: …Publisher.
Kindle. Or …Publisher. https://doi.org/…. Cite chapter or section numbers
in the text when the e-book has no stable page numbers.

### Thesis or dissertation

Surname, Given Name. Year. "Title of Dissertation." PhD diss., University
Name. URL or repository identifier if there is one.

The title is in quotation marks, not italics, because it is unpublished.

### News or magazine article

Surname, Given Name. Year. "Article Title." *Publication*, Month Day.
URL.

In the text, a news article without an author can be cited by publication
name and date, with no list entry, when the venue allows it.

### Book review

Reviewer Surname, Given Name. Year. Review of *Book Title*, by Author Name.
*Journal* volume (issue): pages. DOI.

### Web page

Organization or Author. Year. "Page Title." Site Name. Month Day.
URL.

Give an access date only when there is no publication or revision date,
or when the venue requires one.

### Social media

Cite in the text only, unless the post is central to the argument. Before
citing any post by a participant or a member of the public, read
`ethnographic-sources.md`: a quoted post is searchable, and the citation can
identify its author.

### Video or podcast

Creator or Host. Year. "Episode or Video Title." *Series or Channel Name*.
Platform or Network, Month Day. Video or podcast, running time. URL.

Cite a specific moment in the text with a timestamp: (Host 2023, 14:32).

### Ethnographic film

Director Surname, Given Name, dir. Year. *Film Title*. Distributor.
Running time.

Archival and community film often lacks some of these; give what the
copy carries and say where it is held.

### Personal communication

Text only, no list entry: (Ana Ruiz, personal communication, March 3,
2024), or "in a conversation in March 2024, Ana Ruiz explained…". For
people you met as research participants, read `ethnographic-sources.md`
before using any name.

## Converting from notes and bibliography

1. Turn each note into a parenthetical (Author Year, page). Notes that
   carried commentary as well as a citation stay as notes, with a
   parenthetical inside them.
2. Turn the bibliography into a reference list: move the year to follow the
   names, and change the periods and commas around it.
3. Delete any "ibid." and shortened-note forms; author-date has no
   equivalent.
4. Add a and b suffixes where one author has two works in one year.
5. Reconcile: every parenthetical has exactly one list entry, and every list
   entry is cited.
