# Adjudication and the Record

The adjudication runs in isolation from the context that proposed the claim.
That context argued for the claim being worth testing and is invested in the
answer.

The adjudication adds no new objections. Its job is to stage what the
readings produced, deduplicate it, and hand back a worklist the researcher
can act on without being told what to think.

## Report order

The order is load-bearing. Each section answers a different question, and
putting the worklist first buries the two findings that matter most.

### 1. Convergence

Where all three positions arrived at the same defect from different
directions.

Independent arrival is what makes a convergent finding worth acting on first.
Three positions reaching one sentence by three different routes is evidence
about the sentence. Three positions agreeing because the roster was built
from one axis is evidence about the roster.

State the routes, not only the conclusion. "All three object to this
sentence" is weaker than "the ontological position reaches it through levels
of description, the political-economic position through ownership, and the
classificatory position through what the category buries."

**Convergence is never reported as proof.** Name the roster in the same
breath, so the researcher can see what agreed.

### 2. Objections from inside the home position

If a home position was named, report separately any objection its reading
raised against the claim.

This is usually the sharpest finding in a run, and the reason is structural:
an objection arriving from inside the researcher's own commitment cannot be
answered by discounting where it came from. An objection from a position the
researcher does not hold can always be met with a prior disagreement about
the position. An objection from their own cannot.

Report it plainly and do not soften it. Softening here is the failure the
separate section exists to prevent.

If no home position was named, say so rather than omitting the section.

### 3. What stays open

A genuine conflict that no wording fixes.

State it as a question with the positions named on each side and what each
side costs. Never dissolve it, never recommend a side, and never let a
conflict quietly become a defect because a defect is easier to write.

The test for whether something belongs here: could the researcher resolve it
by editing a sentence? If yes, it is a defect. If it requires them to decide
what they believe, it stays open.

### 4. The worklist

Two visibly distinct kinds. The distinction is the point, and the
adjudication may never convert one into the other.

**Defects.** Something in the material is wrong or contradicts itself, with
the evidence attached. State these flatly. A sentence attributing to a system
what the argument locates in a relationship is a defect: the researcher's own
argument is the evidence.

**Decisions.** A judgment the researcher has to make. State these as the
question plus what each answer costs. Never as an instruction.

The failure mode is writing a decision as though it were a defect, because
the imperative mood is shorter and reads as more useful. "Decide whether the
criterion belongs to the system or to the relationship" is a decision.
"Fix the criterion so it belongs to the relationship" is the same judgment
with the researcher removed from it.

Rank defects above decisions, and within each kind rank by what turning on
it would change.

## The record

```markdown
# Rival interpretations: [claim, short form]

Material: [path or description]
Claim under test: [as confirmed by the researcher]
Roster: [three positions] — home position: [name, or "none named"]
Positions that produced no reading: [name and why, or "none"]
Date: [date]

## Convergence
- [Finding, with the route each position took to it]

## From inside the home position
- [Objection, or "no home position named"]

## What stays open
- [The question, the sides, and what each costs]

## Defects
| # | Where | What | Evidence |
|---|-------|------|----------|

## Decisions
| # | The question | What each answer costs |
|---|--------------|------------------------|

## Resolution
[The researcher's decision on what stays open, in their words, with their
reason. Or: "Carried forward unresolved as of [date]."]

## Unresolved
- [What was not settled, and what is blocking each.]

## Assembled rather than authored
- [Anything drafted on request rather than decided by the researcher.]
```

## Closing the record

The record is not finished when the adjudication reports. It is finished when
the Resolution section is filled by the researcher.

An unresolved record is a legitimate finished state, and "carried forward
unresolved" with a date is a real entry. What is not legitimate is an empty
Resolution section presented as a completed test, because it reads as though
the instrument settled something.

Keep the Unresolved list. A record with nothing unresolved after a full pass
has usually been tidied rather than finished.

## Why the record is worth keeping

A methods section can say that a central interpretation was tested against
three named analytical positions, that the positions converged on one point
and divided on another, and that the author resolved the division a
particular way for a stated reason. That is a claim about the research
process that a reader can evaluate.

The record is the evidence for it, and it is the reason this skill produces a
document rather than a conversation.

Two honesty constraints on any such account. Name the roster, because a
convergence claim is uninterpretable without knowing what agreed. And say
that the readings were machine-argued, because a reader who assumes three
colleagues read the chapter has been misled by omission.
