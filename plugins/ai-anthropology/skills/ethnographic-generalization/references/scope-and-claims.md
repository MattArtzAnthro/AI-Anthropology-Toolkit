# Scope, Confidence, and the Claim Record

The warrant says why the claim holds; the scope says where. This reference
covers stating scope conditions a reader can use, calibrating confidence
honestly, the claim record artifact, and the ethics of a claim that has
grown larger than its consent.

## Scope Conditions

Scope conditions state what the claim depends on, so a reader — including
the reader Lincoln and Guba (1985) made responsible for judging transfer —
can assess fit to their own case. Work through five axes; not every axis
binds every claim, but each should be considered and the binding ones
stated:

| Axis | The question | Example of a condition specific enough to fail |
|---|---|---|
| **People** | Whose practices does the claim describe — and which positions within the setting? | "Couriers paid per delivery, not salaried dispatch staff" |
| **Place** | What about the site does the pattern depend on? | "Dense urban delivery zones where couriers wait together between orders" |
| **Institutions** | Which organizational or regulatory arrangements must be present? | "Platforms that publish individual performance metrics to workers" |
| **Historical moment** | What conjuncture is the claim inside? | "Post-2020 labor oversupply; pre-algorithmic-transparency regulation" |
| **Network position** | How is the case connected to the wider assemblages the claim gestures at? | "A chapter of a transnational movement — not an isolated instance of one" |

Two tests separate genuine scope conditions from the limitations
boilerplate that reviewers skim past:

- **The falsification probe.** "Name a case where you would expect the
  claim *not* to hold, and say why." The answer becomes a scope condition.
  Inability to answer is diagnostic: the claim is unfalsifiable, or it has
  not been thought through as a claim.
- **The fit-judgment test.** Could a reader with a candidate receiving
  context actually use the stated conditions to decide whether the claim
  applies to them? "Findings may not generalize to other contexts" fails
  this test; every entry in the table above passes it.

Scope conditions are commitments, not disclaimers. They are also where an
apparent weakness becomes an asset: the connectedness of multi-sited cases,
the singularity of the site, the researcher's partial position — stated as
conditions, these make the claim usable; buried, they make it fragile.

## Confidence and the Uncertainty Ledger

Assign each claim a confidence level with reasons. The level is the
researcher's judgment; the reasons are what make it inspectable.

- **High.** The warrant ran the full chain: disconfirmation searched and
  dispositioned, at least one rival seriously engaged, comparison
  disciplined, checks done where warranted. The claim would survive a
  skeptical reader who had the fieldnotes.
- **Moderate.** The pattern is robust in the corpus but a named part of
  the warrant is thin — a rival not fully discriminated, a scope axis
  unresolved, member checking not feasible. Name the thin part.
- **Provisional.** A working hypothesis worth stating and testing —
  abductively generated, not yet returned to the field or the corpus for
  checking. Provisional claims are legitimate ethnographic products;
  mislabeled ones are not.

The uncertainty ledger, one row per generalizing claim:

| Claim | Kind | Evidence basis | Rival addressed | Scope conditions | Confidence and reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

A ledger with the same confidence in every row is a form that was filled,
not a set of judgments. The ledger should make visible which claim the
researcher would defend most fiercely and which they would quietly drop
under pressure — if it does not, ask those two questions directly and
revise it.

## Claim Language by Kind

The written claim should let a reader hear what kind it is. Calibrated
openings, to adapt rather than copy:

- **Analytic:** "This case complicates X's account of ___: where the theory
  predicts ___, the fieldwork shows ___, suggesting the proposition holds
  only where ___."
- **Theoretical:** "From this material I develop the category of ___,
  constituted by ___ and ___, to name a process that existing vocabularies
  split apart."
- **Transferability:** "In this setting, under the conditions described,
  ___. I offer this as a working hypothesis for settings that share ___;
  the description above is deliberately dense enough to let readers judge
  that fit."
- **Middle-range:** "The mechanism I call ___ links ___ to ___ through
  ___. It should be observable wherever ___, and suppressed where ___."
- **Case-to-case:** "For practitioners in structurally similar ___, three
  lessons follow, conditional on ___."

The one register rule that never bends: uncertainty survives the audience.
A policy brief, client readout, or op-ed gets the same scope conditions and
uncertainty statement as the monograph, in plainer words — "we saw this
across [settings]; it is most likely to apply where [conditions]; what we
are least certain about is [uncertainty]." Communication changes with
audience; evidential standards do not. Compression belongs to the
conference-materials and public-engagement skills; what may never be
compressed away is stated here.

## The Claim Record

The artifact the session produces. One record can carry several claims;
each claim carries its own kind, scope, and confidence.

```markdown
# Claim Record: [project name]

Stance: [primary, secondary]
Field configuration: [single site / multi-sited / comparative / digital]
Analysis inputs: [themes confirmed on date, codebook version]

## Claim 1
[The claim, one to three sentences, in the researcher's words.]

Kind: [analytic | theoretical | transferability | middle-range |
      case-to-case] — via [within-case / extended case / direct], because
      [one line]

### Inferential target
This finding speaks to [ ] wherever [ ] holds.

### Evidence base
- [Themes and segments, with source IDs and codebook version]
- [Events, documents, complements]

### Emic-to-etic translations ratified
| Emic term | Etic construct | What is lost |
|---|---|---|

### Disconfirmation log
| Negative case | Decision (revise / bound) | Consequence |
|---|---|---|

### Rivals
| Rival | Discriminating evidence | Disposition |
|---|---|---|

### Comparison discipline
[Where similarity does warrant work: diffusion / common context /
co-variation, with reasoning.]

### Scope conditions
Holds where: [conditions, by axis]
Expected to fail where: [the falsification probe's answer]

### Confidence
[High / moderate / provisional] because [reasons; the thin part named].

## Ethics at scale
[Consent scope vs claim scope; identifiability and stigma at the new
reach; dissemination decisions and any withheld formulations.]

## Unresolved
- [Kept, not tidied. These are the agenda for the next pass or the next
  fieldwork.]

## Assembled rather than authored
- [What the machine drafted on request — candidate translations, scope
  candidates, formatted tables — as distinct from what the researcher
  decided.]
```

## Ethics of Scaling Up

A claim that broadens becomes more consequential for the people it
describes, and scale-up therefore requires more reflexive caution, not
less (Abu-Lughod 1991). The professional obligations that constrain
generalization directly (American Anthropological Association 2012):
openness about purpose and methods, consent as an ongoing dialogue rather
than a signature, protection of records, and dissemination that does not
create risk, enable misuse, or compromise confidentiality.

Concretely, before any claim record is handed to writing:

- **Identifiability rises with reach.** A pattern attributed to "a
  hospital" in a dissertation becomes attributable to *the* hospital when
  the finding circulates. Re-run the anonymization judgment at the claim's
  new audience, not its old one — the research-writing skill's
  anonymization guidance operates downstream of this check.
- **Stigma attaches to categories.** A generalized claim about a community
  can re-stigmatize it in ways the situated finding did not. Ask who could
  use this claim, for what, against whom.
- **Consent has a scope.** Participants consented to a study; they did not
  necessarily consent to becoming the evidentiary base of a broad public
  claim. Where the generalized claim exceeds the consent conversation,
  the repair belongs to the informed-consent skill (re-consent, community
  consultation) or the claim narrows. If a generalized claim increases
  identifiability, re-stigmatizes, or exceeds what participants consented
  to disclose, the stronger methodological claim is still the weaker
  anthropological act.

Withholding a formulation is a legitimate scholarly decision and belongs in
the record — "Ethics at scale" documents what was not claimed and why,
which is part of the account, not a gap in it.
