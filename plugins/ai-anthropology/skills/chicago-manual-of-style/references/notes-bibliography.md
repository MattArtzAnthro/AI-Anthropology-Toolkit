# Notes and Bibliography: Patterns for Anthropology

Chicago 18th-edition notes and bibliography. Used in historical anthropology,
in edited volumes from humanities-leaning presses, and wherever sources are
archival or need commentary. Check `editions-and-venues.md` first if the
venue has its own style sheet.

Each source appears up to three times: a full note on first citation, a
shortened note after that, and a bibliography entry. Number notes
sequentially through the document (or through each chapter, if the venue
restarts numbering by chapter). The examples below show one note number
each only to illustrate form.

## The shape of each form

| Element | Full note | Shortened note | Bibliography |
|---|---|---|---|
| Names | Given name first | Surname only | Surname first for the first author |
| Separators | Commas, publication facts in parentheses | Commas | Periods |
| Pages | The specific page cited | The specific page cited | Article page range; none for books |
| Indentation | First line indented | First line indented | Hanging indent |

## Book

1. Clifford Geertz, *The Interpretation of Cultures: Selected Essays* (Basic
Books, 1973), 12.

2. Geertz, *Interpretation of Cultures*, 14.

Geertz, Clifford. *The Interpretation of Cultures: Selected Essays*. Basic
Books, 1973.

No place of publication (18th edition). A shortened title is four words or
fewer, drops an initial article, and drops the subtitle.

## Edited volume and chapter

3. Mary Louise Pratt, "Fieldwork in Common Places," in *Writing Culture: The
Poetics and Politics of Ethnography*, ed. James Clifford and George E.
Marcus (University of California Press, 1986), 33.

4. Pratt, "Fieldwork in Common Places," 35.

Pratt, Mary Louise. "Fieldwork in Common Places." In *Writing Culture: The
Poetics and Politics of Ethnography*, edited by James Clifford and George E.
Marcus. University of California Press, 1986.

"Ed." in a note becomes "edited by" in the bibliography. The chapter's page
range is no longer required in the bibliography entry (18th edition).

## Translated book with original date

5. Marcel Mauss, *The Gift: The Form and Reason for Exchange in Archaic
Societies*, trans. W. D. Halls (Routledge, 1990), 33. Originally published
in French in 1925.

Mauss, Marcel. *The Gift: The Form and Reason for Exchange in Archaic
Societies*. Translated by W. D. Halls. Routledge, 1990. Originally
published in French in 1925.

## Journal article

6. Sherry B. Ortner, "Theory in Anthropology since the Sixties,"
*Comparative Studies in Society and History* 26, no. 1 (1984): 144,
https://doi.org/10.1017/S0010417500010811.

7. Ortner, "Theory in Anthropology," 150.

Ortner, Sherry B. "Theory in Anthropology since the Sixties." *Comparative
Studies in Society and History* 26, no. 1 (1984): 126–66.
https://doi.org/10.1017/S0010417500010811.

Notes give the cited page; the bibliography gives the article's page range.
The issue is "no. 1" in this system, against "(1)" in author-date.

## Archival collection

8. Field diary, 14 June 1951, box 3, folder 12, Name of Papers, Name of
Repository, City.

9. Field diary, 14 June 1951, Name of Papers.

Name of Papers. Name of Repository, City.

The bibliography lists the collection, not each item. Put the item, its
date, and its location in the note. Follow the repository's preferred
citation, which most finding aids state. Read `ethnographic-sources.md`
before citing anything in a collection that holds participants' or
communities' materials.

## Dissertation, news, web, media

The components are those in `author-date.md`, rearranged: in a note, given
name first, commas between elements, the date toward the end; in the
bibliography, surname first, periods between elements. For example:

10. Given Name Surname, "Title of Dissertation" (PhD diss., University Name,
Year), 87.

Surname, Given Name. "Title of Dissertation." PhD diss., University Name,
Year.

## Personal communication

Note only, never the bibliography:

11. Ana Ruiz, conversation with the author, March 3, 2024.

For research participants, read `ethnographic-sources.md` first.

## Ibid. and shortened notes

Chicago discourages "ibid." in favor of shortened notes, which survive
reordering and are easier to check. Some presses still want ibid.; follow
the press. Never mix the two practices in one manuscript.

## Discursive notes

Anthropology uses notes for asides: a translation, a gloss on a local term,
an account of how a quotation was recorded. Keep these, and put any
citation inside them in the note form above.

## Converting from author-date

1. Replace each parenthetical with a superscript note number at the end of
   the clause or sentence, after the punctuation.
2. Write the first citation of each source as a full note and later ones as
   shortened notes.
3. Turn the reference list into a bibliography: move the year to the end of
   the publication facts, and change the punctuation.
4. Remove a and b year suffixes; titles now distinguish the works.
5. Reconcile: every note's source is in the bibliography, and every
   bibliography entry is cited, unless the venue wants a bibliography of
   works consulted.
