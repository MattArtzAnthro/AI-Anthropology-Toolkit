# Rival Interpretations

Test one contestable claim against readings argued from other analytical
positions, and hand back what stays open.

This is a validity practice, in the family of triangulation, negative case
analysis, and rival explanations. Its payoff is that the objection met here
is usually the one a reviewer would have raised, but that is the consequence
rather than the purpose. The purpose is to find out whether an interpretation
survives contact with positions that had no stake in producing it.

It is not a review of a document. It targets one claim, and it declines more
often than it runs.

This skill adopts the Friction by Design conventions at Tier 1; the canonical
form of the sections below is defined in [skills/DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md).

## What This Skill Will and Will Not Do

A rival reading is an argument about the researcher's own material. Which
reading to keep is the judgment they defend in print.

**Will not do, under any setting.** Choose which claim is under test without
the researcher confirming it. Resolve what the positions leave open. Certify
a claim as successfully defended. Report convergence as proof. Decide that a
rival reading is wrong because it is inconvenient for the argument. Supply
ethnographic or archival material that is not in the source to make a rival
reading work. The first four are the researcher's judgments and an
interpretation whose defense came from elsewhere is one they cannot answer
for. The last is not a judgment at all; it is fabrication, and no depth
setting enables it.

When asked for any of these directly, propose options and ask which. Do not
decide, and do not lecture about why not.

**Will do, on request.** Propose the claim under test and say why it looks
load-bearing. Propose the roster and say what each position has at stake.
Run the readings and the rebuttals. Report convergence, what stays open, and
the worklist. Assemble the record from the researcher's own resolution.
Format any deliverable. Anything produced this way is marked in the output.

## Calibrating the Depth

Friction should be proportional to what the researcher could plausibly get
wrong, not applied at uniform depth to everyone. Ask once, at the start of
the engagement:

> "Do you want a full pass, where I stop at the claim under test and again at
> what stays open, or an advisory pass, where I run the test and report?"

**Full pass.** The skill stops at the ratification gate and again before the
record closes. For a first article, a claim the researcher has not argued in
public before, or material where they suspect they are finding what they
expected.

**Advisory pass.** For a researcher who has argued this before and arrives
with the claim already stated sharply. Confirm the claim in one exchange,
run, and let them direct what follows.

The ratification gate is stopped at under both settings. An advisory pass
calibrates how much is discussed, not whether the researcher gets to say what
their own claim is.

If the setting was already asked for this engagement — by the advisor that
dispatched this skill — use it and do not re-ask.

Default to asking. Do not infer the setting from how confident the researcher
sounds.

## Workflow

### Step 1: The Gate

Four conditions. All four must hold before anything runs, and the judgment is
stated rather than made silently.

1. **It is a reading, not a fact.** Attribution, dates, provenance, and
   sources are errors to correct. They have no opposing side and no rival
   position will produce one.
2. **It is load-bearing.** Reverse the reading. If nothing downstream
   changes, it does not earn three readers.
3. **The positions would diverge.** This is a prediction and it can be
   wrong. Where a multi-lens coding run already exists, its friction points
   are evidence rather than prediction, and that is the strongest available
   form of this condition.
4. **The researcher has committed.** A reading still forming is ossified by
   this rather than tested.

When the gate fails, say which condition failed, name the rival reading in a
sentence anyway, and route: prose and craft to the research-writing skill, an
argument that has not formed to the paper-planning skill, a method question
to the methodology-selection skill.

Declining is the common outcome. A skill that always finds something worth
three readers is selling, not testing.

### Step 2: Propose the Claim and the Roster. Gate.

Read [references/roster-selection.md](references/roster-selection.md).

One question carrying both, because they are one decision:

```
Claim under test: [the reading, stated in the researcher's own terms]
Load-bearing because: [what reversing it costs the argument]

Roster:
  [position] — [what it has at stake in this material]
  [position] — [what it has at stake in this material]
  [position] — [what it has at stake in this material]

Also candidates, not being tested: [other contestable claims, ranked by
what reversing each would cost]

Confirm, change the claim, or change the roster.
```

Confirming the claim outranks confirming the roster. A weak position costs
one voice; the wrong claim costs the run, and it also tells the researcher
what their central claim is, which is not this skill's to decide.

The candidates not being tested are reported because that list is a map of
where the argument is exposed, and it costs nothing.

**A proposed claim is not a settled one.** Nothing downstream proceeds until
the researcher has confirmed or moved it. If they have not answered, the last
thing on the screen is the question.

### Step 3: Ask for the Home Position

One optional question: which of the three is the researcher's own
commitment. Not a ranking of all three.

Its job is not tie-breaking. It is that an objection arriving from inside the
researcher's own position cannot be discounted by discounting its source, and
the adjudication reports those separately for that reason.

If no home position is named, the test runs flat and the record says so.

### Step 4: Read in Isolation

Read [references/reading-brief.md](references/reading-brief.md).

Dispatch one subagent per position. Each receives the material, its assigned
position, and the claim under test. **None sees the others' readings.**

Isolation is the whole reason this dispatches subagents. Three positions
argued in one context produce one context talking to itself in three
registers, and the divergence that results is stylistic rather than
theoretical.

**No reading is admitted without a falsifier drawn from the material.** Not a
theoretical objection: a statement of what would have to be true in the text,
the data, or the instrument for the reading to fail. A position with nothing
at stake here cannot produce one, and the honest outcome is that it drops out
and the record says which position produced nothing and why.

### Step 5: Rebut, Where There Is Something to Rebut

A rebuttal fires only where two readings make incompatible claims about the
same span. Firing every pair spends a reader on an exchange that produces one
sentence of agreement.

**No rebuttal without a span.** Quote the claim being attacked and point at
the passage that defeats it. Arguing about a paraphrase wastes the one
advantage this method has.

### Step 6: Adjudicate

Read [references/adjudication.md](references/adjudication.md).

Dispatch the adjudication in isolation from the context that proposed the
claim, which is invested in it. Report in this order: convergence, objections
from inside the home position, what stays open, then the worklist.

The worklist carries two visibly distinct kinds. **Defects** carry their
evidence and are stated flatly. **Decisions** are stated as the question plus
what each answer costs. Never convert a decision into a defect by phrasing it
as an instruction.

What stays open is stated and never dissolved. It gets no recommended
resolution.

### Step 7: Close the Record

The record is not finished when the adjudication reports. It is finished when
the researcher's resolution is written into it, with their reason, or
recorded as carried forward unresolved.

That closing step is what makes this a research artifact rather than a
critique: evidence for a methods section that a claim was tested against
named positions and resolved deliberately.

Keep the unresolved list. A record with nothing unresolved after a full pass
has usually been tidied rather than finished.

### Step 8: Hand Off

- **Revising the prose that carries the claim**: the research-writing skill.
- **An argument that keeps breaking in the same place**: the paper-planning
  skill, because a tested claim inside an unsettled argument is still
  unsettled.
- **Answering an actual reviewer**: the academic-review skill.
- **What the findings generalize to once the reading holds**: the
  ethnographic-generalization skill.

## Guardrails

- **Never run without the researcher confirming the claim.** Testing the
  wrong claim well is worse than declining, because it arrives looking like
  a result.
- **Never present convergence as proof.** Three positions agreeing is
  evidence about the positions as much as about the claim, and a roster
  chosen badly converges by construction.
- **Never manufacture disagreement.** Positions that genuinely agree here are
  reported as agreeing. The falsifier and span rules exist to make the
  difference visible.
- **Never invent material to make a rival reading work.** A rival reading is
  argued from the source or it is not argued.
- **Never resolve what stays open.** Naming a seam and then recommending a
  side converts the researcher's decision into the machine's.
- **Never let a declined gate produce a debate anyway.**

## Common Failure Modes

| Failure mode | Prevention |
|---|---|
| Three paraphrases of one reading, presented as divergence | Isolation in round one, plus the falsifier admission rule |
| A position with nothing at stake padding out the roster | It cannot produce a falsifier from the material, so it drops and the record says so |
| The instrument choosing the researcher's central claim for them | Step 2 is a gate, and confirming the claim outranks confirming the roster |
| Friction added to look rigorous | Declining is the common outcome, and the gate states which condition failed |
| The gate that becomes a form | The candidates not tested are reported, so a researcher who confirms without reading has visibly skipped something |
| Decisions restated as instructions | The worklist's two kinds are structurally distinct and the adjudication may not convert between them |
| Convergence read as vindication | Reported as convergence, never as proof, and the roster is named in the record |
| A record tidied into completeness | The unresolved list is kept |

## Examples

**Example 1: A claim that earns the test**

Input: "My chapter argues that the criterion I built is what separates a
genuine research partnership from mere mutual adjustment, and my two cases
fall on either side of it."

Output approach: gate passes on all four conditions. Propose the claim and a
roster of three positions with distinct holds on it, plus the two other
contestable claims in the chapter that are not being tested. After
ratification, run three isolated readings, each with a falsifier drawn from
the chapter. Discover that all three independently object to one sentence
that attributes to a system what the argument locates in a relationship. Fire
one rebuttal on the only pair that collides on the same span. Report the
convergent defect, the objection from inside the researcher's own position,
and the one thing that stays open, which is whether the criterion measures a
capacity or a recognition. Record their resolution.

**Example 2: A claim that does not**

Input: "Can you run this on my methods section? I want to be sure it holds."

Output approach: the gate fails condition two. The methods section describes
what was done, and reversing any of it changes the account rather than the
argument. Say so, name the one place a rival position would actually press,
which is the sampling rationale, and route the rest to the research-writing
skill. Do not run.

**Example 3: Evidence rather than prediction**

Input: "My cross-lens comparison shows the lenses disagreed most on the
chunks about kinship obligation, and my theme three rests on those chunks."

Output approach: condition three is satisfied by measurement rather than
guess, which is the strongest form this gate takes. Propose the theme's
warrant as the claim under test and build the roster from the positions that
actually diverged in the coding run rather than from a fresh reading of the
material. Run, and report whether the coded disagreement survives being
argued out or dissolves into a vocabulary difference.
